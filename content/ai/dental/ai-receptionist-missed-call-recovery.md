# AI Receptionist & Missed-Call Recovery for Dental Clinics

An AI receptionist for a dental clinic is a system that answers or immediately follows up on calls the front desk can't take — during lunch, between patients, or after hours — and either books the appointment directly or captures the details for staff to close. It exists because most dental practices lose new-patient calls simply because the phone rings while someone is already on another call or the front desk is closed.

## How It Works
Input: Inbound calls and voicemails, current appointment availability from the practice management system
Processing: The system answers or calls back, identifies the reason for the call (new patient, existing patient, emergency, billing question), and checks real scheduling rules
Output: A booked appointment, a routed message to the right staff member, or an emergency escalated immediately by phone or text

## Use Cases
- Capturing new-patient enquiries that call after hours or during peak lunch-hour call volume
- Booking routine appointments directly against real-time chair availability
- Triaging and escalating true dental emergencies to on-call staff instead of letting them go to voicemail
- Answering common questions (hours, insurance accepted, location) without tying up a staff member
- Logging every call outcome back into the practice record, so nothing gets lost between shifts

## Benefits
- Directly recovers new-patient calls that would otherwise go to a competitor
- Removes the "stuck on one line, missed the next call" problem inherent to a single front-desk phone
- Extends effective office hours without extending staff hours
- Reduces interruptions to clinical and hygiene staff who currently field overflow calls

## FAQ
### What happens with a real dental emergency after hours?
Emergency calls are identified and escalated immediately to whoever is on call, by call transfer or text — the system doesn't try to handle clinical judgment calls itself.

### Does it sound like a real person or an obvious bot?
It's built to handle the routine 80% of calls (scheduling, hours, insurance questions) smoothly and hand off anything unusual to a human, rather than trying to fake a full conversation on every call.

### How many calls does a typical dental practice actually miss?
Front desks answering a single phone line routinely miss calls during patient check-in/out and lunch coverage gaps — exact volume varies by practice size and call volume, which is part of what a system like this is set up to measure.

### Can it text patients instead of calling them back?
Yes — for many practices a text follow-up converts better than a voicemail callback, and the system can do either depending on what the practice prefers.
